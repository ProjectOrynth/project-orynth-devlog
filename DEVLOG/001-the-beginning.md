# Devlog 001 – The Beginning
**23 November 2026**

*We create the world. They create the future.*

## So, what is Project Orynth?

I've been thinking about this idea for a while, and I've finally decided to try and make it happen.

The basic idea is quite simple, although the actual project probably won't be!

I want to create an artificial species, give it a world to live in, and see what happens when we leave it alone.

Not a game, not a story with a scripted ending, and not a civilisation simulator where we tell everyone what to do.

I want to create something that feels alive.

The inhabitants should have their own needs, personalities, memories and experiences. They should be able to explore their surroundings, learn from what happens to them, interact with each other and hopefully develop things we never anticipated.

I have absolutely no idea how far we'll get, but that's part of the appeal.

## The inhabitants

The plan is to start small. Very small, actually.

Five individuals. Generation 001.

They'll belong to a fictional species, and at this stage I haven't even settled on exactly what they'll look like.

They'll have different physical characteristics and personalities, but I don't want to give them predefined roles or tell them how they're supposed to behave.

They'll need to eat, drink, sleep, stay healthy and avoid danger. Beyond that, I want to give them the ability to be curious, experiment with things and learn from their experiences.

Perhaps one will become particularly interested in certain objects. Another might be more cautious. Maybe they'll form relationships, develop habits or discover ways of doing things that we never considered.

We won't know until we let them loose.

## The world

Their starting environment will be called **The Cradle**.

I'm imagining a natural valley with forests, rivers, open areas, wildlife and plenty of things to discover.

But I don't want it to be just a pretty background.

The ecosystem needs to function independently. Plants should grow and reproduce, animals should have their own lives, predators should hunt, and resources shouldn't magically replenish whenever they're needed.

I'd also like to simulate some of the environmental systems that make life possible, such as oxygen, carbon dioxide, sunlight, water cycles and soil nutrients.

Ideally, the inhabitants will eventually be able to change their surroundings too.

If they cut down trees, build huts, start farming or create settlements, those changes should actually affect the world.

## What might happen?

This is probably the part I'm most excited about.

I don't want to program a list of technological milestones and wait for them to unlock.

If they discover fire, that's great. If they work out how to build a shelter, even better. Maybe they'll eventually develop agriculture, tools, metalworking or something we haven't even thought about.

But perhaps they won't.

They might struggle to survive, fail to pass on knowledge or even go extinct.

I'm not going to force progress just to make the experiment interesting.

I'd rather watch them do absolutely nothing remarkable for months than manufacture discoveries that never actually happened.

## How will it work?

The intention is to run the simulation locally on my own PC, using free software and local AI models.

I'm also fairly new to programming, so this is going to be a learning experience in its own right.

We'll build it gradually, starting with the basics and adding complexity as we go. I'm not expecting to create an entire artificial civilisation overnight!

Visually, I'm thinking of a top-down world that we can observe, with the ability to inspect individuals and see what's happening around them.

And importantly, this isn't something we pause when we're not watching. The intention is for the world to continue running 24/7.

## Where we are today

Nowhere, technically speaking!

There isn't a world yet, there are no inhabitants, and nothing has been simulated.

For now, it's an idea, a name and a rather ambitious plan.

I'll be documenting the development here, including the successes, mistakes, problems and unexpected results along the way.

Hopefully, one day we'll be able to look back at this first entry and see just how far things have come.

**Five individuals. One world. No predetermined future.**

Let's see what happens.
